How it works

From one long recording to a folder of finished tracks.

1

Record

  • Arm it and it starts when the music does, with a little pre-roll.
  • Tracks are marked as they play, named from what Windows says is playing, and grouped into albums.
  • Browse, zoom and fix marks while it records, then jump back to live. No break in the recording.
  • Records any output (what the PC plays) or any input.
2

Albums & cuts

  • Cuts should already be in the right place. Audition each start and end in a couple of seconds, then tick it.
  • Zoom right down to the sample with the mouse wheel; Ctrl+wheel makes quiet parts big.
  • Gap and dropout detection, with a quick way to deal with each one.

Waveform or spectrogram

  • Press W to see the same join as a spectrogram, handy for spotting where a fade really ends.
  • The source check shows where the spectrum stops, so you know roughly what quality you recorded.
3

Fill gaps & covers

  • Cover art comes straight from Windows media info, so often there is nothing to do.
  • MusicBrainz suggests tags and covers. Accept them in bulk or ignore them.
4

Export

  • FLAC, WAV, MP3 or AAC, with each album on its own output profile if you like.
  • Folder and file names from a template, like {albumartist} - {album}/{track:00} {title}.
  • A final report to check before you export. Keep the session to re-export later, or delete it when you’re done.

About the developer

Hello, I’m Mat, from London. I’m a music grad, pianist, and a bit of a serious music fan.

I wrote this app primarily for me, but I hope you like it too.

Computer Science was my first degree and I’ve been coding since 1988. But you may have guessed AI tools were involved somewhat in the production of WinTapeRecorder’s code. Please don’t let that put you off. I’m a critical and careful user of AI, and value human decisions far more than computer-made ones. And so my approach is this: human-led design and human-checked code.

If something is wrong, or could be improved, please let me know in GitHub Issues.

More about me on my blog, Mat’s Hideout.

Download WinTapeRecorder
Please use it responsibly: it’s made for capturing recordings you already own. It records what Windows sends to your speakers, the same way Audacity does. It doesn’t unlock or decrypt anything, and if an app blocks recording, it just records silence.
